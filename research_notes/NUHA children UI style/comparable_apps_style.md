# Comparable children's learning and special-needs apps: visual and interaction style (desk research, as of Oct 2026)

Scope note: this is desk research only (store listings, vendor sites, reviews, and a few academic or evaluation reports). The web searches returned text, not screenshots, so palette and illustration descriptions are thin wherever no text source states them. Those cases are listed under Gaps instead of being guessed. Vendor claims and independent evidence are labelled separately throughout.

## 1. Per-app style profile (palette, illustration, characters, type, motion, sound, rewards, pre-reader navigation, parent area, sensory settings)

### Takeaway
The best-known mainstream apps share four traits. They use animal or monster mascots, run without ads, are navigated by voice and pictures rather than text, and keep the parent area behind a gate that pre-readers cannot pass. Special-needs and AAC apps work differently. They stress stable grids and consistent symbol positions for motor planning, user-controlled sound, and customisation with personal photos and recorded audio, rather than characters and spectacle.

### Cited Findings

**Khan Academy Kids (Khan Academy; built by the former Duck Duck Moose team)**
- Characters: an animal cast. Kodi the Bear leads, with Ollo the Elephant (phonics and letter sounds), Reya the Red Panda (stories and writing), Peck the Hummingbird (numbers) and Sandy the Dingo (puzzles, memory, problem-solving). Each character is tied to a subject — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/khan-academy-kids/id1378467217)
- Age range is 2–8 (also labelled "Made for Ages 0–5"). The listing says "100% free, no ads, no subscriptions." It has Parent/Teacher Settings, for example to turn off seasonal themes, plus Teacher Tools for assignments and progress tracking — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/khan-academy-kids/id1378467217)
- Reward: reviewers mention children "collecting the prizes," but the listing does not describe the reward mechanics. The listing mentions no music/sound toggle (it only notes Super Simple Songs sing-alongs) — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/khan-academy-kids/id1378467217)
- I found no official art-direction statement. One SEO-style page describes "vibrant illustrations" and gender-neutral characters, but it is low quality and should not be relied on — [search summary; KA blog](https://blog.khanacademy.org/?p=74)

**Sago Mini / Toca Boca (sister studios; Sago Mini is Toronto-based)**
- Philosophy: open-ended play with "no instructions, no rules to follow" — [Sago Mini letter to parents](https://sagomini.com/article/sago-mini-letter-to-parents/). The products avoid "artificial incentives, formal rules, or scripted characters" so that children own their play — [Sago Mini CEO message](https://sagomini.com/article/message-from-the-ceo/)
- Sago Mini Trips has been described as having no high scores, no "Game Over" screens and no ticking clocks ("digital toys, not digital chores") — [Screenwise review](https://screenwiseapp.com/media/sago-mini-trips-game); [Screenwise, Sago philosophy](https://screenwiseapp.com/media/sago-mini-puppy-preschool-app)
- Sago Mini Jinja's Garden (Apple Arcade, 3D, preschool):
  - It has no words anywhere. Story and instructions come through pictures, and tasks are signalled with routine-style checkmark icons.
  - A virtual joystick confused toddlers in testing, so the team replaced it with tap-to-move.
  - It has no stamina meter, no timers, no crops dying, and unlimited seeds.
  - More than 100 children played it during iterative testing, and it has no ads or in-app purchases — [gori.me](https://gori.me/en/apps/ipad-app-news/167337/)
- Toca Boca's design ethos has been characterised as poking the environment "to see what happens" — [search summary of reviewer commentary; Joan Ganz Cooney Center Q&A](https://joanganzcooneycenter.org/2014/11/14/qa-with-sago-sagos-jason-krogh/)

**Endless Alphabet / Endless Reader (Originator)**
- Mechanic: a monster's mouth holds words in alphabetical order. The child drags letters, which make their phonetic sounds, and then watches a short animation that acts out the word's meaning (for example, a monster growing "gargantuan").
- There are no goals or objectives; children "work at their own pace."
- The parent info sits behind a screen that non-readers can't get past.
- It needs an internet connection and a lot of storage. The free Android version requires a paid unlock.
- Common Sense rates it 3+, with Ease of Play "a lot" and Educational Value "some" — [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/endless-alphabet)
- Common Sense's learning rating praises the animated definitions but notes there are "no checks for understanding" — [Common Sense Education](https://www.commonsense.org/education/app/endless-alphabet)
- Originator describes itself as the creator of the Sesame Street "Monster at the End of This Book" app — [search summary; Horn Book review](https://hbook.com/story/endless-alphabet-app-review)

**Lingokids**
- Children pick activities from a main menu organised by subject. Activities include short videos, mazes, puzzles and tracing. Common Sense's headline: "a big mix of early learning activities that can get pricey" — [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/lingokids-play-and-learn)
- Parent reviews praise engagement but complain about the subscription, including unclear trials and unexpected charges — [Common Sense parent reviews](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/lingokids-play-and-learn/user-reviews/adult)
- A 2020 review of an older version found "no advertising or product placement," and stickers appear among its features — [Australian Council on Children and the Media](https://childrenandmedia.org.au/app-reviews/apps/lingokids)

**Duolingo ABC**
- It covers preschool to Grade 2 and has more than 700 lessons. Stories read words aloud and highlight each word as it is spoken. It launched free in 2020 — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/MX/app/id1440502568); [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2020/03/26/duolingo-abc-learn-to-read-ios-free-app/)
- The main Duolingo app, not ABC, won the 2023 Apple Design Award for Delight and Fun. Its VP of Design said "Fun is the most important part of the work we do" — [Madhyamam](https://english.madhyamam.com/technology/innovative-design-duolingo-wins-apple-design-award-2023-1169335)

**PBS KIDS games**
- PBS KIDS requires producers to check game UI for accessibility: screen-reader metadata, text-to-speech, and text-to-background contrast — [Parenting Special Needs](https://www.parentingspecialneeds.org/article/pbs-kids-focus-is-on-content-accessibility-for-all/)
- Cyberchase "Railway Hero" (WNET with Bridge Multimedia) offers customisable text size, colour and contrast options, separate volume for music, sound effects and voiceover, and keyboard play with audio description — [Parenting Special Needs](https://www.parentingspecialneeds.org/article/pbs-kids-focus-is-on-content-accessibility-for-all/)
- A Cyberchase game, "Echo Explorers," was designed for children who are blind, deaf, autistic, have ADHD, or have motor impairments — [Connected Learning Summit 2021](https://2021.connectedlearningsummit.org/event/cyberchase-echo-explorers-exploring-accessible-game-design-for-all-children/)

**Lamsa (Arabic, UAE)**
- Recognition:
  - The UN World Summit Award for Arabic e-content.
  - Apple's "Best New Apps & Games" for children after its 2013 launch.
  - Etisalat Award "Digital Book App of the Year" in 2017.
  - Per twofour54, it has passed one billion minutes of children reading — [UAEBBY](https://uaebby.org.ae/en/2019/01); [twofour54](https://twofour54.com/en/media-centre/press-releases/lamsa-achieves-one-billion-minutes-of-children-reading-edutainment-content); [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/en/2014/07/twofour54-and-lamsa-a-partnership-to-support-arabic-content-for-children)
- An ArabNet profile is titled "Lamsa Creates Arabic Content for Children, with Children," which implies co-design with children. The article itself returned a 404, so I could not confirm the details — [ArabNet](https://www.arabnet.me/english/editorials/entrepreneurship/startups/lamsa-creates-arabic-content-for-children-with-children)

**Feed the Monster (Arabic; CET / Apps Factory / IRC; later Curious Learning)**
- It was built for EduApp4Syria. The Arabic version launched in March 2017, and Curious Learning has since localised it into many languages (at least 25 by Norad's count) — [Norad](https://norad.no/en/front/thematic-areas/education/innovation/eduapp4syria/feed-the-monster-available-in-25-languages)
- The mechanic is feeding letters to a monster that the child raises (a monster-mascot pattern similar to Endless Alphabet) — [INEE resource](https://inee.org/resources/feed-monster)

**Abjadiyat (Alef Education, UAE)**
- It is free Arabic literacy for ages 3–8, with a scaffolded approach and content aligned to Ministry of Education curricula.
- Lessons combine songs, videos and interactive activities, followed by end-of-lesson quizzes.
- Teachers can assign content from the library, and children see how many assignments they have done and how many remain — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1235509585)
- It was originally an Ibdaa/Modhesh World initiative (2017 press release) — [Albawaba](https://www.albawaba.com/index%2Ephp/business/pr/modhesh-world-showcases-%E2%80%98abjadiyat%E2%80%99-educational-app-pre-schoolers-995996)

**Otsimo (special education; Turkey)**
- It targets ages 3–9 and accounts for children who are older but developmentally younger. The premium tier has more than 80 games plus AAC — [The Autism Dad](https://theautismdad.com/2019/06/23/review-otsimo-is-a-fantastic-educational-app-for-kids-with-autism/)
- It is based on ABA. A caregiver screening tailors the content, and a passcode-protected parent section tracks goals, progress reports and play history — [Univ. at Buffalo HAPPE](https://ubwp.buffalo.edu/happe/2021/01/13/otsimo/)
- It is ad-free "to reduce distractions" and recommends games based on the child's performance — [BadCredit.org](https://www.badcredit.org/news/otsimo-offers-game-based-special-education-apps/)
- A vendor claim, which the vendor describes as machine-learning based, is that it adapts difficulty — [Educational App Store](https://www.educationalappstore.com/app/otsimo-special-education-aba)

**Proloquo2Go / Proloquo (AssistiveWare; AAC)**
- It offers 23 grid sizes, from 9 to 144 buttons. Clinicians can adjust button size and spacing, grid count, selection method, and colour scheme and contrast.
- It supports switch scanning and VoiceOver, and includes more than 27,000 SymbolStix symbols plus personal photos — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id308368164)
- Core words stay in consistent locations to build motor planning and muscle memory — [AssistiveWare blog](https://assistiveware.com/blog/proloquo-or-proloquo2go)

**Avaz AAC (India; now owned by PRC-Saltillo)**
- It has graded vocabulary at three levels and consistent word placement for motor memory.
- Users can switch between picture and keyboard modes, with predictive text and more than 40,000 SymbolStix pictures.
- It supports six Indian languages — [Avaz](https://avazapp.com/products/avaz-aac-landing-page); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avaz_app)
- In October 2025 it became a wholly owned subsidiary of PRC-Saltillo, which described it as "design-forward" and multilingual — [Easterseals Tech](https://www.eastersealstech.com/2025/07/01/avaz-aac-app-allows-for-authentic-expression/); [search summary](https://www.taiwannews.com.tw/news/6227131)

**Leeloo AAC**
- It is card-based PECS/AAC with a vector image for each word. Tapping a card offers phrase options, which a TTS voice reads aloud.
- It is aimed at children from 4 and comes with pre-configured preschool and elementary card sets.
- It offers more than 10 voices and 27–28 languages (sources differ). The core is free, with Premium card packs on top — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1508952198); [Easterseals Tech](https://eastersealstech.com/2023/11/09/leeloo-aac-app)
- It was reportedly removed from Google Play at one point (APK available from the developer) — [Spoken AAC](https://spokenaac.com/best-aac-apps/)

**Choiceworks (Bee Visual; visual schedules)**
- It has four board types: Schedule (now/next), Waiting, Feelings, and Feelings Scale.
- Users can add their own photos, recorded audio and video. It comes preloaded with more than 180 images and audio, and supports multiple child profiles — [App Store](https://apple.co/22AcLjP); [Utah visual schedules guide](https://edps.ed.utah.edu/school-psych/_resources/documents/grants/autism-training-grant/Visual-Schedules-Practical-Guide-for-Families.pdf)
- Caregivers, teachers and therapists use it with verbal and non-verbal autistic children and children with ADD — [BridgingApps](https://bridgingapps.org/bridgingapps-bee-visual-app-giveaway-autism-acceptance-month/)
- It is often the first app SLPs and OTs introduce, according to a 2026 roundup written by a competitor's founder — [VizyPlan](https://vizyplan.com/blog/best-visual-schedule-apps-autism-2026)

**Miracle Modus (sensory)**
- It shows "hypnotic rainbows and soft bells." Tapping changes the mode, and settings can change or disable the sounds.
- An autistic developer made it to reduce sensory overload. It is free and iPad-oriented — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id555904748)
- A third party describes it as calming music with a "lovely rainbow of colours" — [Sourcekids](https://sourcekids.com.au/7-apps-to-help-with-sensory-processing-and-self-regulation/)

**Speech Blubs**
- Its core mechanic is video modelling: children imitate videos of other children (company literature review) — [Speech Blubs proof of concept (PDF)](https://cdn.speechblubs.com/2022/10/Design-Proof-of-Concept-1.pdf)

**Tiimo (visual planner; recent entrant)**
- It was a finalist in the 2024 Apple Design Awards in the Inclusivity category. For 2025 it was a finalist for iPhone App of the Year (its English page) or the winner (its French page and LinkedIn); the sources conflict.
- Features include AI breaking tasks into steps and visual timers for transitions — [Tiimo](https://www.tiimoapp.com/resource-hub/tiimo-finalist-2025-app-store-awards); [TWiT](https://twit.tv/posts/tech/how-tiimo-makes-task-and-time-management-easier-neurodiverse-users)

### Inferences
- Mainstream apps for pre-readers lean on mascots, voice-over and icon-only menus. The Sago Jinja's Garden case shows a no-text design working, and a toddler-tested switch to tap-to-move. Both fit a touch-first tablet app for ages 2–6 well.
- AAC apps show that adjustable grid density, button size, spacing and contrast are standard expectations in special-needs software. A NUHA settings panel for educators could adopt the same controls.

### Gaps
- No text source described the actual palettes, illustration techniques (flat vs textured vs 3D) or typefaces for Khan Kids, Lingokids, Duolingo ABC, Lamsa, Abjadiyat or Otsimo. Someone needs to look at the store screenshots directly.
- Lumi was not researched. Avaz's Arabic support is unconfirmed, and so is Otsimo's Arabic support.
- The sensory toggles (mute music, reduce motion) in Khan Kids, Lingokids, Lamsa and Abjadiyat were not documented in the sources I found.

## 2. Evidence, awards, and what reviewers or parents of autistic children praise or criticise

### Takeaway
Independent efficacy evidence is thin. The only controlled studies found were a small RCT of Khan Academy Kids and a quasi-experimental evaluation of Feed the Monster's Arabic version, which called its evidence "weak but encouraging." No independent efficacy trial was found for Otsimo or Speech Blubs. Awards are mostly design or editorial recognitions, not efficacy evidence.

### Cited Findings
- **Khan Academy Kids RCT (UMass Amherst):** about 49–50 preschoolers from low-income families were randomised to Khan Academy Kids or to non-literacy apps for 10 weeks.
  - Children using the app at least an hour a week rose from the 34th to the 47th percentile on the Test of Preschool Early Literacy, and from the 23rd to the 47th percentile on phonological awareness.
  - Print vocabulary showed no significant gain. The results were described as preliminary, and the study was published in the Journal of Children and Media — [UMass](https://www.umass.edu/news/article/new-educational-app-shows-promise); [EdWeek](https://www.edweek.org/leadership/khan-app-boosted-early-literacy-parent-teaching-in-small-trial/2020/05); [Salford blog](https://blogs.salford.ac.uk/cognitive-development-lab/2021/01/27/can-preschoolers-literacy-skills-be-improved-by-using-an-educational-app-a-new-study/)
- **Khan Academy Kids awards:**
  - Apple Editors' Choice.
  - Common Sense Top Rated Educational App.
  - Children's Technology Review Editor's Choice (an Excellence in Design award with a 100% rating, per release notes).
  - Parents' Choice Gold.
  - A KAPi (for the team's Duck Duck Moose work) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/khan-academy-kids/id1378467217); [APKMirror release notes](https://apkmirror.com/apk/khan-academy/khan-academy-kids/khan-academy-kids-2-3-release)
- **Feed the Monster (Arabic):** the evaluation was a quasi-experimental, longitudinal study by INTEGRATED and NYU's CREATE. It compared children using the app against matched children without access, measuring literacy, psychosocial wellbeing and usability. It concluded the evidence that the games build basic Arabic literacy and wellbeing in Syrian refugee children was "weak but encouraging" — [EduApp4Syria summary (PDF)](https://inee.org/sites/default/files/resources/EduApp4Syria-IE-Summary-2018_0.pdf); [Full report (PDF)](https://inee.org/sites/default/files/resources/Feed-the-Monster-IE-Report-2018_0.pdf)
- **Sago Mini Jinja's Garden:** won the 2026 Apple Design Award for Interaction — [gori.me](https://gori.me/en/apps/ipad-app-news/167337/)
- **Duolingo (main app):** won the 2023 Apple Design Award for Delight and Fun. I found no award for ABC specifically — [Madhyamam](https://english.madhyamam.com/technology/innovative-design-duolingo-wins-apple-design-award-2023-1169335)
- **Tiimo:** 2024 Apple Design Award finalist for Inclusivity, and 2025 App Store Awards finalist or winner (sources conflict) — [Tiimo](https://www.tiimoapp.com/resource-hub/tiimo-finalist-2025-app-store-awards)
- **Lamsa:** UN World Summit Award, Apple Best New, and Etisalat Digital Book App of the Year 2017 — [UAEBBY](https://uaebby.org.ae/en/2019/01); [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/en/2014/07/twofour54-and-lamsa-a-partnership-to-support-arabic-content-for-children)
- **Otsimo:**
  - It claims Mom's Choice, Parent's Pick, and the HundrED Global Collection 2020–2022 (self-reported) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/otsimo-special-education-1/id1084723774)
  - Its parent testimonials (for example, a nonverbal child learning letters "in two weeks") are anecdotal, vendor-hosted marketing — [Otsimo reviews page](https://otsimo.com/en/special-education-speech-therapy-reviews/)
  - I found no peer-reviewed efficacy study — [search summary](https://ubwp.buffalo.edu/happe/2021/01/13/otsimo/)
- **Speech Blubs:** no independent app-specific trial was found. Video modelling in general has three decades of support for teaching social, academic and functional skills — [Education and Treatment of Children (2007 review)](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/223589)
  - A cautionary data point: a 2023 thesis tested commercially made video models with three autistic participants, and only one imitated the modelled skill — [MSU thesis](https://d.lib.msu.edu/etd/51177?q=W+P)
- **Endless Alphabet:** Common Sense praises its memorable animated definitions but criticises the missing comprehension checks, the internet and storage demands, and the Android paywall — [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/endless-alphabet); [Common Sense Education](https://www.commonsense.org/education/app/endless-alphabet)
- **Lingokids:** parents criticise unclear trials and unexpected subscription charges — [Common Sense parent reviews](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/lingokids-play-and-learn/user-reviews/adult)
- **Miracle Modus:** the praising testimonials on its store page come from people connected to the developer, and the developer admits the first-launch benchmark is unreliable — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id555904748)
- **Sensory design guidance (autism):**
  - One guide recommends muted colours and reports that people with ASD show a significantly lower preference for bright red and yellow. It advises simple layouts and avoiding unnecessary motion — [Manchester Digital](https://www.manchesterdigital.com/post/code-computerlove/how-to-design-for-autism)
  - Research based on literature review, case studies and focus groups (no controlled trial) stresses soothing colours with adequate contrast and warns that colour can trigger overload and anxiety — [Benha Univ. paper (PDF)](https://fapa.stafpu.bu.edu.eg/Advertisement/7661/publications/Mona%20Emam%20Amin%20Aly_Employing%20Inclusive%20UX%20design%20Considerations%20in%20Color%20for%20Autisti.pdf)
  - In a Malaysian autism game study, user testing suggested reduced overstimulation, but the methodology is not detailed — [UUM JCISC 2024 (PDF)](https://repo.uum.edu.my/32110/1/JCISC%2003%202024%20101-113.pdf)
  - A review of cognitive accessibility in educational games recommends offering stimuli through several channels, letting players adjust the pace, and keeping content simple — [UAB repository](https://ddd.uab.cat/record/318546?ln=en)

### Inferences
- The strongest marketing claims (Otsimo, Speech Blubs) have the weakest independent evidence. NUHA should avoid claiming effectiveness until it has its own evaluation, ideally modelled on the Feed the Monster quasi-experimental design in an Arabic context.
- Parent complaints cluster on monetisation (subscriptions, paywalls) more than on visuals. Ad-free and IAP-free status is itself praised (Khan Academy Kids, Otsimo, Apple Arcade titles).

### Gaps
- I did not find systematic autism-parent community feedback (for example, Reddit or forums) about sensory overload in specific mainstream apps such as Khan Kids, Lingokids or PBS. This needs targeted community research.
- I found no Kidscreen or BAFTA Kids award data for these apps in this pass.
- An Alfaisal University usability evaluation of Arabic apps for autistic children exists, but only its title was retrieved — [Alfaisal](https://faculty.alfaisal.edu/awabil/publications/a-usability-evaluation-of-arabic-mobile-applications-designed-for-children-with-special-needsautism)

## 3. Recurring patterns: best-regarded mainstream apps vs special-needs apps

### Takeaway
Mainstream leaders put play first. They use mascots, voice-led navigation, low-pressure open-ended play without timers or "game over," no ads, and a gated parent area. Special-needs apps put predictability and control first: consistent layouts, adjustable grids and contrast, photo and audio personalisation, now/next structure, adult-tailored content, and sound that can be disabled.

### Cited Findings
- **Low-pressure play:**
  - Sago Mini has no rules or artificial incentives; Sago Mini Trips has no timers and no game-over screens — [Sago Mini](https://sagomini.com/article/message-from-the-ceo/); [Screenwise](https://screenwiseapp.com/media/sago-mini-trips-game)
  - Jinja's Garden has no stamina, no timers and no failure states — [gori.me](https://gori.me/en/apps/ipad-app-news/167337/)
  - Endless Alphabet has "no objective or goals beyond exploring" — [Common Sense](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/endless-alphabet)
- **Pre-reader navigation:** Jinja's Garden uses no words at all, with icon checkmarks for tasks — [gori.me](https://gori.me/en/apps/ipad-app-news/167337/). Duolingo ABC reads aloud and highlights each word as it is spoken — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/MX/app/id1440502568)
- **Mascot-led subjects:** Khan Kids assigns a character to each domain — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/khan-academy-kids/id1378467217). The monster is a recurring literacy mascot in both Endless Alphabet and Feed the Monster — [Common Sense](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/endless-alphabet); [INEE](https://inee.org/resources/feed-monster)
- **Gated parent areas:**
  - Endless Alphabet hides parent info behind a gate that non-readers can't pass — [Common Sense](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/endless-alphabet)
  - Otsimo uses a passcode-protected parent section with goals and progress reports — [UB HAPPE](https://ubwp.buffalo.edu/happe/2021/01/13/otsimo/)
  - Khan Kids has Parent/Teacher settings and teacher assignment tools — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/khan-academy-kids/id1378467217)
- **Special-needs specifics:**
  - Consistent symbol placement for motor planning in Proloquo2Go and Avaz — [AssistiveWare](https://assistiveware.com/blog/proloquo-or-proloquo2go); [Avaz](https://avazapp.com/products/avaz-aac-landing-page)
  - Adjustable grid size (9–144 buttons), spacing, contrast and switch access in Proloquo2Go — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id308368164)
  - Now/next schedule boards, waiting boards, feelings scales, and own photos and recorded audio in Choiceworks — [App Store](https://apple.co/22AcLjP)
  - Caregiver screening that tailors content in Otsimo — [UB HAPPE](https://ubwp.buffalo.edu/happe/2021/01/13/otsimo/)
  - Disableable sound in Miracle Modus — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id555904748)
- **Accessibility settings in mainstream apps:** PBS's Railway Hero has separate music, SFX and voiceover volumes plus text size and contrast options — [Parenting Special Needs](https://www.parentingspecialneeds.org/article/pbs-kids-focus-is-on-content-accessibility-for-all/)

### Inferences
- NUHA could combine the two families. The child-facing layer would follow mainstream patterns: voice-led, icon-only, a mascot, no failure states. The adult-gated layer would follow special-needs patterns: per-child profile and screening, separate volume channels, reduced motion, adjustable target size and contrast, and now/next activity sequencing with custom photos and audio.
- Separate audio sliders for music, SFX and voice (as in Railway Hero) are a concrete, low-cost sensory setting with precedent.

### Gaps
- I found no source comparing reward systems (stickers, collectibles, stars) for their effect on autistic or ADHD children.

## 4. What Arabic apps do differently (RTL, fonts, culture)

### Takeaway
The Arabic apps found (Lamsa, Abjadiyat, Feed the Monster, and the Arabic autism apps Aawn, BabNoor and Tawasol) mainly differentiate on language and curriculum. That means MSA vs dialect choices, Ministry-aligned content, Arabic symbol sets and teacher assignment tools. Design-level documentation of RTL layout or Arabic typography for children is largely absent from public sources.

### Cited Findings
- **Abjadiyat:** aligned with Ministry of Education curricula, with teacher assignment and homework tracking. Its content team spans educators, artists and linguists — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1235509585)
- **Lamsa:** the ArabNet headline says it is built "with children" (details unconfirmed). Its distribution partnerships include OSN — [ArabNet](https://www.arabnet.me/english/editorials/entrepreneurship/startups/lamsa-creates-arabic-content-for-children-with-children); [Arab News](https://arabnews.com/node/1213166/)
- **Feed the Monster:** built originally in Arabic for Syrian refugee children, then localised outward — [Norad](https://norad.no/en/front/thematic-areas/education/innovation/eduapp4syria/feed-the-monster-available-in-25-languages)
- **Arabic autism apps:**
  - BabNoor (du, UAE) adapts its content to several Arabic dialects — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/lifestyle%2Fcommunity%2Fsmart-app-helps-children-with-special-needs-1.1981513)
  - Tawasol (Qatar, Mada) is Arabic symbol-based AAC — [Nafath/Mada](https://nafath.mada.org.qa/nafath-article/tawasol-app-an-arabic-augmentative-and-alternative-communication-solution/)
  - Aawn is a PECS-based Arabic prototype whose authors note most autism apps are in English — [TEM Journal via CEEOL](https://www.ceeol.com/search/article-detail?id=1168172)
  - A Saudi (Majmaah University) 2024 paper reviews educational apps for autistic children in Arab countries; only its metadata was retrieved — [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.4018%2FIJICTE.359439)
- **Typography and RTL:**
  - Choosing a typeface that matches local visual expectations matters more than resizing. Apple's guidance to add about 2pt for RTL is a pragmatic but simplistic start, and the Apple and Google guides don't fully cover RTL complexity — [AHFE/CMS conference paper](https://openaccess.cms-conferences.org/publications/book/978-1-964867-19-9/article/978-1-964867-19-9_24)
  - Many fonts are not optimised for Arabic, which leaves spacing uneven — [Smartling](https://de.smartling.com/blog/translate-rtl-languages)
  - Directional icons and comparison symbols need mirroring, and numbers and brand names create bidirectional runs — [Localazy](https://localazy.com/blog/6-challenges-of-localizing-your-app-to-arabic-and-how-to-solve-them)
  - The choice between dialect and MSA depends on whether an app targets one country or the whole region — [Localazy](https://localazy.com/blog/6-challenges-of-localizing-your-app-to-arabic-and-how-to-solve-them)

### Inferences
- For Saudi children, a dialect question arises for voice-over (Saudi or Najdi/Hijazi dialect vs MSA). MSA aligns with curriculum, as Abjadiyat does, while dialect may suit the youngest or language-delayed children, as BabNoor's dialect adaptation suggests.
- Arabic AAC and symbol sets exist (Tawasol), but apps such as Avaz, Leeloo and Proloquo2Go have no confirmed Arabic support. A well-designed Arabic-only app for special needs may face little direct competition, though this needs checking against current store listings.
- Swipe and progress directions, "next" arrows, and number display (Arabic-Indic vs Western digits) are decisions NUHA must make explicitly. The sources show these are common failure points.
